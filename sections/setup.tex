\section{Evaluation Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\label{sec:problem}

Given a passage $x$ and ordered candidates $C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$,
admission stores $K\subseteq C$ with $|K|\leq k$. The future question
$q$ and answer $a$ are hidden. When $q$ arrives, the frozen reader sees
only $q$ and $K$; the original passage has left its window. Every policy
receives the same candidates and budget. We measure three successive
outcomes: within-episode ranking, retention of the useful concept, and
downstream recall.

\paragraph{Within-Episode Ranking.}
Within-episode area under the receiver operating characteristic curve
(AUC) measures how well a policy orders candidates that compete for
memory. It is the fraction of useful--distractor pairs ordered correctly,
with half credit for ties, averaged across episodes. For useful concept
$b_i$ in episode $i$, score $s$ and $n$ episodes,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
A_{\mathrm{episode}}={}&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|C_i|-1}\\
&\times\sum_{c\in C_i\setminus\{b_i\}}\Big[\mathbf{1}\{s(b_i)>s(c)\}\\
&\qquad\quad+\tfrac12\,\mathbf{1}\{s(b_i)=s(c)\}\Big].
\end{split}
\label{eq:episodeauc}
\end{equation}
Pooled AUC compares candidates across the entire battery and summarizes
score discrimination.

For the provenance analysis, we replace utility labels with statedness
labels. The resulting \emph{provenance AUC} measures discrimination of
stated from unstated candidates. Statedness is determined by a
case-insensitive stem match against the passage.

\paragraph{Useful Concept Retention.}
Bridge retention is the fraction of episodes whose stored set contains
the construction-labelled useful concept:
\begin{equation}
B_k=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{b_i\in K_i\}.
\label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
This measures whether the useful concept survives the memory budget.
A policy can rank it above most distractors and still omit it at a
small budget. The intended-bridge reference prioritizes the intended useful
concept and provides a reference for this construction.

\paragraph{Downstream Recall.}
Recall is the fraction of future questions that a fixed, frozen reader
answers correctly from the stored concepts. A retained useful concept
can still lead to an incorrect answer. Correct answering also depends
on the reader and the other stored concepts.

The reader answers greedily, with Qwen3 thinking disabled. A blind
semantic judge receives only the question, gold answer and response.
It accepts a committed correct answer or exact equivalent. A separate
string-match grader checks agreement (App.~\ref{app:judge}).

Question-only and whole-passage readers establish how much of the answer
can be recovered before admission and with the complete original input,
respectively.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
We compare admission policies under the same inputs and memory budget.
Baselines include the untrained yes/no importance report, a 1-10 importance rating,
the reversed yes/no importance report, admit what the passage omits,
omission then report, wording variants, and the provenance-adjusted report.
We also compare the combined teacher used directly, random selection,
output likelihood, the workspace readout, and students trained from
either teacher signal alone. All receive the passage with the future
question hidden (App.~\ref{app:baselines}).

Paired AUC differences use 4,000-draw whole-episode bootstraps and 95\%
intervals. Recall comparisons use exact McNemar tests on paired answers
and seed-averaged differences with paired 95\% bootstrap intervals
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}). Trained results give three-seed means and sample
standard deviations. Bold table entries identify SDA.
