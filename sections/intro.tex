\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An LLM agent must choose what to remember before it knows the next
question, and the useful concept is often an inference that the passage
never states. A description of a landmark can identify a city without
naming it, so remembering that city preserves the link to a later
question about the local language. Once the passage leaves the context,
the agent's answer depends on which inferences survived admission to
memory.

Memory systems make this choice through importance judgments: the
model's importance report, its 1--10 importance rating
\citep{park2023generative}, or published admission criteria built on
context support \citep{amac2026,consistencygate2026}. In each case, the
passage provides the evidence for admission, but an agent also needs
to preserve what it inferred from the passage. This creates a gap
between recognizing an inference during reading and selecting it for
later use: a memory system can discard the useful concept even when
the model has identified it.

The importance report is the model's yes/no answer to whether a
candidate is among the most important things to remember, scored by
$P(\mathrm{yes})$ against $P(\mathrm{no})$. Controlled pools fix each
episode's useful concept and vary which candidates are stated
(present in the passage), so a drop in area under the ROC curve (AUC)
isolates the effect of statedness. The standard pool uses the
generator's original candidate lists, where the useful concept is
usually the only unstated candidate; in the statedness-balanced pool,
the passage states the useful concept in half the episodes. The
report's pooled AUC falls from 0.880--0.897 among unstated candidates
alone to 0.658--0.736 on the statedness-balanced pool and 0.322--0.427
on the standard pool, ranking useful concepts below chance against
stated distractors. The 1--10 importance rating and published criteria
built on context support also favor stated candidates.

The model's workspace readout, the peak reciprocal rank of a candidate
under the logit lens across layers, measures the candidate's presence
in the model's workspace, the verbalizable concepts it is currently
reasoning with \citep{anthropic2026workspace}, and ranks the useful
concept above chance on the same standard pool. The model's
intermediate layers therefore encode information that its importance
report does not express. Naming the useful concept in an otherwise
unchanged episode raises AUC for both signals, with a larger increase
for the workspace readout (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}).

Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) transfers the model's candidate rankings
into its admission decision (Figure~\ref{fig:sda}). The teacher is a
frozen model's ranking of each episode's candidates by workspace
readout and output likelihood (the candidate's mean token
log-probability given the passage); SDA labels the top two for
admission and trains a low-rank adaptation (LoRA) adapter
\citep{hu2021lora} on those labels through the model's importance
prompt. Optimization uses only the passage, candidates, and labels
generated by the model.

SDA improves answer accuracy over existing memory systems' importance
judgments. On the primary statedness-balanced pool, it exceeds each
model's own importance report in 54 of 54 comparisons across models,
seeds, and memory budgets, and exceeds the 1--10 importance rating in
52 of 54 (Table~\ref{tab:balanced}). With two stored concepts, Qwen3-8B
gains 10.7 accuracy points over its importance report and 5.2 over the
rating. ConsistencyGate, A-MAC, and Nemori fall below the untrained
report. SDA exceeds these published admission criteria by 14.1--27.8
points at the same memory budget
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}).

On the primary statedness-balanced pool, removing the workspace
readout from the teacher lowers retention of unstated useful concepts
by 6.6 points at one stored concept, in all five models
(Table~\ref{tab:teacherretention}). The paired-passage set, in which
two episodes contain the same candidates in different orders but
differ in passage, question, and useful concept, tests the teacher's
use of the passage because only the passage identifies the useful
concept. Across five models, removing the workspace readout from the
teacher lowers answer accuracy by 1.9 points at one stored concept
(Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}).

SDA is a plug-and-play adapter that an off-the-shelf serving engine
applies only to admission requests. On the standard pool, the served
Qwen3-8B adapter keeps 50.1\% answer accuracy at 242 candidates per
second (Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stated-content preference in importance judgments.}
    Importance judgments order candidates by statedness, a preference
    that controlled pools separate from usefulness, while the workspace
    readout tracks unstated useful concepts (\S\ref{sec:gap}).
    \item \textbf{Plug-and-play SDA module.} SDA distills the workspace
    readout and output likelihood into the model's importance prompt
    through an adapter that serves only admission requests
    (\S\ref{sec:method}).
    \item \textbf{Results across models and memory settings.} Evaluation
    across six models from three families, three seeds, three memory
    budgets, and five candidate pools establishes answer accuracy gains
    over the importance report, the rating, and published admission
    criteria, while ablations show that the workspace readout's
    contribution concentrates on unstated useful concepts and that both
    teacher signals contribute to answer accuracy on the paired-passage
    set (\S\ref{sec:recall}, \S\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}
