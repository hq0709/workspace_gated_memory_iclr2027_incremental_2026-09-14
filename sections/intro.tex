\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An LLM agent must decide what to store before it knows the next question.
The most useful memory is often an inference the passage never states: a
passage about a landmark can identify a city without naming it, and
remembering that city preserves the link to a later question about the
local language. Once the passage leaves the context, the answer depends on
which inferences were kept.

Memory systems admit candidates through importance judgments the model
expresses, such as ratings and admit-or-discard decisions
\citep{park2023generative}, or through criteria built from what the
context supports \citep{amac2026,consistencygate2026}. All of these read
what the passage says. An inference the passage leaves unstated has the
least support from any of them.

We find that the ordering of a model's importance report follows
provenance: whether the passage states a candidate. Across six models,
useful-concept AUC is 0.322--0.427 on the standard pool, where the useful
concept is usually the unstated candidate, and 0.658--0.736 on the
statedness-balanced pool, where it is stated in half the episodes. Naming
the useful concept in an otherwise unchanged episode raises mean
workspace-readout AUC from 0.563 to 0.956 and the report's from 0.373 to
0.504 (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}). Admission criteria built on context support
show the same ordering: single-pass ConsistencyGate scores correlate $+0.90$ with
statedness on the statedness-balanced pool, and its own threshold admits
26.7\% of useful candidates on the standard pool.

The model's internal computation carries the inference its report does not
express. Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) distills that computation into the
admission decision: a frozen model ranks candidates by its workspace
readout and output likelihood, labels its two highest-ranked candidates,
and trains one low-rank adapter \citep{hu2021lora} on its own importance
prompt. Supervision comes from the model's processing of the passage and
candidates, with no future question, reward or external teacher. The
adapter runs in a standard serving engine and scores only admission
requests.

On the statedness-balanced pool, SDA exceeds each model's own importance
report in all 54 model--seed--budget comparisons across six models and
exceeds the 1--10 importance rating in 52 of 54
(Table~\ref{tab:balanced}); for Qwen3-8B at two stored concepts, SDA
improves recall by 10.7 points over the model's report and 5.2 points over
the rating. Admission criteria from ConsistencyGate, A-MAC and Nemori fall
below the untrained report on this pool, and SDA exceeds these criteria by
14.1 to 27.8 points at two stored concepts
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). On a battery where only the passage
identifies the useful concept, removing the workspace readout from SDA's
teacher lowers recall by 1.9 points at one stored concept across five
models (Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}). The served Qwen3-8B adapter
reaches 50.1\% recall on the standard pool at 242 candidates per second
(Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Provenance governs importance judgments.} The model's own
    importance report and admission criteria built on context support order
    memory candidates by provenance; the model's internal computation tracks the
    inference the passage leaves unstated. Controlled pools that balance
    statedness and naming isolate this effect.
    \item \textbf{Self-distilled admission.} SDA is a plug-and-play
    admission adapter distilled from the model's own internal rankings and
    trained without future questions, rewards or an external teacher. It
    serves in a standard engine and applies the learned decision to memory
    admission requests.
    \item \textbf{Gains across models and memory settings.} Evaluation spans
    six models from three families, three seeds, three memory budgets and
    five candidate pools, with gains over the model's own importance report,
    the 1--10 importance rating and admission criteria from published
    methods. Ablations show that both internal signals in SDA's teacher
    contribute to recall.
\end{itemize}
