\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Provenance and Importance Judgments}
\label{sec:gap}

Importance judgments track provenance when stated and unstated
candidates compete. Among unstated candidates alone, the importance
report ranks useful concepts with pooled AUC 0.880--0.897 across six
models, compared with 0.592--0.697 for the workspace readout
(Table~\ref{tab:withinunstated}). When both provenance classes compete
in the statedness-balanced pool, report AUC falls to 0.658--0.736.
On the standard pool, it falls below chance to 0.322--0.427, while
provenance AUC is 0.582--0.688. The importance report discriminates
usefulness within a provenance class and loses that ordering when
provenance classes compete.

The workspace readout ranks useful concepts higher than the importance
report on the statedness-balanced pool, with pooled AUC 0.719--0.812.
Statedness alone provides little useful-concept discrimination in this
pool. A rule that reads only whether the passage states a candidate
has AUC 0.515 pooled and 0.516 within episodes across all 363 episodes.
The within-episode measure tests the competition that admission must
resolve: which candidates from the same episode to store.

Changing what the passage names changes both rankings. Naming the bridge
raises mean workspace AUC from 0.563 to 0.956 and report AUC from 0.373
to 0.504 (Table~\ref{tab:statedcontrol}). Naming a distractor lowers both
on Qwen3-8B (App.~\ref{app:naming}). Wording that says the passage may
never mention the useful concept follows the same pattern. Qwen3-8B
AUC is 0.537 when the useful concept is inferred, 0.417 when named,
and 0.621 on Explicit (App.~\ref{app:wording}).

Omission identifies the useful concept as the sole unstated candidate
in 95\% of standard-pool episodes. At $k=2$, admit what the passage omits
and omission then report both reach 53.4\% recall; reversed yes/no
report reaches 46.6\%. Balancing provenance lowers recall for all three
policies because omission also favors distractors. All three then rank
below the importance report at $k=2,3$; omission then report exceeds it
at $k=1$ (Tables~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}, \ref{tab:baselines}
and~\ref{tab:paired}).

SDA trails omission then report on the standard pool by $9.5$ points at
one stored concept. At two stored concepts, the difference is $-3.4$
points. On the statedness-balanced pool, SDA exceeds this rule by $14.6$
points at two stored concepts
(Tables~\ref{tab:paired} and~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}).

\subsection{Admission Results}
\label{sec:recall}

Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) exceeds each model's own importance report
in all 54 model--seed--budget comparisons on the statedness-balanced
pool. At $k=2$, Qwen3-8B recall rises from 43.0\% to
53.7/54.8/52.6\% across seeds (Table~\ref{tab:balanced}).

The 1-10 importance rating is the strongest untrained admission policy
for Qwen3-8B on this pool. We evaluate it for all six models at all three
budgets. SDA exceeds it in 52 of 54 model--seed--budget comparisons.
The two exceptions are Qwen3-14B at three stored concepts, where two
of three seeds reach 44.9\% against the rating's 45.1\%.

Qwen3-8B SDA reaches 40.5/53.7/51.8\% recall at $k=1,2,3$ on the
statedness-balanced pool. It exceeds implied-content wording, the
provenance-adjusted importance report, the combined teacher used
directly, and direct output likelihood at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). Section~\ref{sec:ablations}
separates the contributions of the teacher's two signals.

At $k=2$, SDA gains $+5.2$ recall points over the 1-10 importance rating.
Its gains are $+9.1$ points over implied-content wording, $+14.6$ over
 the provenance-adjusted importance report, and $+8.8$ over the direct
combined teacher (Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}). Paired policy
differences average seeds.

Published admission criteria also fall below the untrained importance
report on the primary pool. We implemented ConsistencyGate
\citep{consistencygate2026}, A-MAC utility \citep{amac2026}, and Nemori's
prediction-error criterion \citep{nemori2026} from their papers.
Each admits top-$k$ candidates under the same budget protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). At two stored concepts, SDA gains
$+18.7$ recall points over each ConsistencyGate form, $+14.1$ over
A-MAC utility, and $+27.8$ over prediction error.
The smallest gain is $+6.9$ points over A-MAC utility at three stored
concepts (Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}).

ConsistencyGate and A-MAC utility track whether the passage states a
candidate. On the statedness-balanced pool, their correlations with
statedness are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for
sampled ConsistencyGate, and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility. On the standard
pool, ConsistencyGate's own 0.7 threshold admits 26.7\% of useful
candidates against 70.8\% of all candidates. Its within-episode
useful-concept AUC is 0.133 for single-pass scoring and 0.190 for sampled
scoring. These criteria exhibit the provenance ordering found in
importance reports.

A fitted provenance offset reproduces the development pool's omission
regularity. Adding a single offset for stated candidates, fitted on
development episodes, raises development AUC from 0.304 to 0.978.
It lowers statedness-balanced AUC from 0.683 to 0.575. The offset ranks
every unstated candidate ahead of every stated candidate, reproducing
omission then report. Their recalls match at $k=2,3$; at $k=1$, they
reach 36.6\% and 36.9\%, respectively. SDA's ranking
changes with the passage (Figure~\ref{fig:replaced}).

SDA improves retention of inferred useful concepts and retains stated
concepts. For Qwen3-8B at two stored concepts, the importance report
retains the useful concept in 46.4\% of stated episodes and 33.0\% of
inferred episodes. The workspace readout reaches 92.8\% and 35.7\%,
respectively. Across three seeds, SDA reaches 76.8/81.8/72.4\% in stated
episodes and 64.8/64.8/64.3\% in inferred episodes.

The 173 inferred-condition episodes contain no sentence naming any
candidate. SDA reaches 37.4/50.1/46.6\% recall at $k=1,2,3$, compared
with 24.9/40.5/37.0\% for the importance report. The gains are $12.5$,
$9.6$, and $9.6$ points. SDA also exceeds the 1-10 importance rating
by $5.6$ points at two stored concepts. These differences average
three seeds.

On the standard pool, all 18 runs exceed their own importance report,
with a mean gain of 9.6 points
(Tables~\ref{tab:sdabudget} and~\ref{tab:paired}). Semantic and
string-match grading both recover these gains (App.~\ref{app:judge}).

Useful-concept retention supplies information that the frozen reader
uses. SDA improves Qwen3-8B within-episode ranking and retention on the
standard pool (Table~\ref{tab:episodemetrics}). We replay SDA's two
selections on the 249 episodes where it retains the bridge, then
replace the bridge with the next-ranked candidate. Recall falls from
54.6\% to 37.8\%, a paired decrease of 16.9 points.
The replacement loses 59 answers and gains 17.

\paragraph{Reader references and candidate proposal.}
Question-only and whole-passage readers reach 22.9\% and 63.9\% recall
on Qwen3-8B, bracketing SDA's 50.0\% on the standard pool.
With a context-only proposer, SDA reaches 35.5/33.3/33.1\% across
three seeds at $k=2$ (Table~\ref{tab:readerproposal}; proposal prompt
in App.~\ref{app:prompts}).

\subsection{Robustness Across Candidate Pools}
\label{sec:budgets}

SDA's recall gains extend to the naming-balanced pool, the pool with
unstated distractors, and the enlarged pool.
On the naming-balanced pool, SDA exceeds each model's own importance
report in 17 of 18 model--budget cells across six models.
The remaining cell differs by 0.1 points
(Table~\ref{tab:namingrecall}). SDA exceeds the 1-10 importance rating
in all twelve model--budget cells at one and two stored concepts.
At three stored concepts, SDA and the rating are within 2.6 points
for every model. Qwen3-8B also improves within-episode ranking and
useful-concept retention across seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:namingbalanced}). Admission gains persist when naming
carries no utility information.

On the pool with unstated distractors, SDA exceeds each model's
importance report in every model--seed--budget comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:stress}). Here, the useful inference competes with
other unstated concepts. For Qwen3-8B, SDA also exceeds direct output
likelihood at every budget on this pool and the enlarged pool
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:bigpool}).

At $k=2$ on the pool with unstated distractors, SDA gains 9.6 recall
points over the importance report, 4.1 over the 1-10 importance rating,
and 5.8 over omission then report (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).
On the enlarged pool, with 12.41 candidates, the corresponding gains
are 11.0, 4.7, and 6.6 points (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

At one stored concept, SDA trails omission then report by $8.4$ points
on the pool with unstated distractors and by $5.5$ points on the enlarged
pool (Table~\ref{tab:paired}). At two and three stored concepts, SDA leads
every measured baseline on both pools
(App.~\ref{app:poolresults}).

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:scale}

\paragraph{Contribution of the workspace readout.}
Removing either teacher signal lowers recall on the paired-passage
battery, where only the passage identifies the useful concept.
We remove either the workspace readout or output likelihood from SDA's
teacher and train the same adapter on the resulting labels.
Training episodes and the step budget remain fixed.

Each candidate set appears twice, under two passages with their own
questions. The useful concept differs
within each pair. Both episodes therefore include their partner's
useful concept, and only the passage identifies which concept is
useful. We score 181 pairs on Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B,
Qwen2.5-7B, and OLMo-2-7B, yielding 1,810 observations per budget
(Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}; per-model results in
Table~\ref{tab:crosspairmodels}).

At one stored concept, removing the workspace readout costs $+1.9$
recall points and $+7.3$ retention points. Removing output likelihood
costs $+1.3$ recall points; retention rises by $1.4$ points. Both
ablations lower mean recall at every budget.
Removing the workspace readout also lowers retention at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}). Costs are SDA minus the trained
ablation, averaged over three seeds and pooled over models.

\input{tables/tab_crosspair_pooled}

The replaced-passage test measures how the student's selections depend
on the passage (Figure~\ref{fig:replaced}). We score each episode
with its own passage and another episode's passage, keeping candidates,
labels, and the question fixed. On 363 standard-pool episodes with one
stored concept, Qwen3-8B SDA retention falls by $20.9$ points.
The student trained without the workspace readout loses $2.9$ points;
the student trained without output likelihood loses $26.5$ points
(Figure~\ref{fig:replaced}). Without the workspace readout, the
student's ordering barely changes when the passage changes.

On the primary statedness-balanced pool, removing output likelihood
costs $+1.6$ and $+1.7$ recall points at one and two stored concepts.
Removing the workspace readout costs $+0.7$, $+0.6$, and $+0.3$
points at one, two, and three stored concepts

\paragraph{Training the importance interface beyond scoring.}
Training the importance interface improves recall beyond using the
teacher directly. On the standard Fresh-2 pool at $k=2$, SDA reaches
50.0\% recall, compared with 45.7\% for the direct combined teacher
and 40.2--40.5\% for the validation-selected log-odds objectives
(Tables~\ref{tab:sdaablation} and~\ref{tab:objective};
App.~\ref{app:objective}).

A matched wording comparison separates training gains from prompt
changes. The \emph{implied-content wording} is the strongest measured
Qwen3-8B prompt baseline that permits inferences while leaving the
useful concept's provenance unspecified. It raises standard-pool
report AUC from 0.321 to 0.462 and improves recall at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}). Holding this wording fixed for
training and evaluation, SDA reaches 37.1/50.7/51.0\% recall at
$k=1,2,3$. It exceeds the prompt baseline at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}). SDA trained with passage-only wording
reaches 37.6/50.0/52.2\%
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:paired}).

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deployment}

Standard serving preserves SDA recall at 242 candidates per second
on one A100-80GB (Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).
The served Qwen3-8B adapter reaches 50.1\% recall on the standard pool,
within 0.3 percentage points of the reference selections.
Paired disagreements are two versus three episodes. Top-2 memories
agree on 91.7\% of episodes, and admission AUC differs by at most 0.001.

The throughput measurement uses batched serving with prefix caching
over 1,607 candidates after warm-up. SDA takes 4.1\,ms per candidate;
the importance report reaches 290 candidates/s at 3.4\,ms per candidate
(Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}). The adapter serves admission requests.
The frozen model answers from the admitted memory.
