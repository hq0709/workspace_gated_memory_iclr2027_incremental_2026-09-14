\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Importance reports follow the pool's provenance structure}
\label{sec:gap}

The report ranks useful concepts above chance on the statedness-balanced
pool, and the workspace readout ranks them higher still.
Across six models, their pooled AUCs are 0.658--0.736 and
0.719--0.812, respectively. A rule that reads only whether the passage
states a candidate ranks near chance: useful-concept AUC is 0.515
pooled and 0.516 within episodes, over all 363 episodes. The within-episode value is
the relevant test: candidates compete for memory inside each episode.
On the standard pool, report AUC is 0.322--0.427 and provenance AUC
is 0.582--0.688. The report's ordering follows the provenance structure
of the pool it is given. Naming the bridge raises mean workspace AUC
from 0.563 to 0.956 and report AUC from 0.373 to 0.504
(Table~\ref{tab:statedcontrol}); naming a distractor lowers both on
Qwen3-8B (App.~\ref{app:naming}). Among unstated candidates alone,
the report ranks usefulness above the workspace readout across all six
models (App.~\ref{app:withinprovenance}); the full-pool ordering also
reflects competition across provenances.

Omission directly identifies the useful concept, the sole unstated
candidate, in 95\% of standard-pool episodes. At $k=2$, admit what the
passage omits and omission then report both reach 53.4\% recall; reversed
yes/no report reaches 46.6\%. Balancing provenance changes these recalls
to 32.0\%, 39.1\% and 29.8\%: the cue that favored the bridge now also
favors distractors. All three rank below the report in recall at $k=2,3$;
omission then report exceeds it at $k=1$
(Tables~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}, \ref{tab:baselines}
and~\ref{tab:paired}). On the standard pool, SDA trails omission then
report by $9.5$ points at one stored concept ($[-13.7,-5.3]$); the
difference at two is $-3.4$ points ($[-7.0,+0.4]$). On the
statedness-balanced pool, SDA exceeds this rule by $14.6$ points at two
stored concepts ($[+9.4,+19.7]$) (Tables~\ref{tab:paired}
and~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}). The provenance-cued wording that says the
passage may never mention the useful concept follows this pattern:
Qwen3-8B AUC is 0.537 when the concept is inferred, 0.417 when named, and
0.621 on Explicit (App.~\ref{app:wording}).

\subsection{SDA retains useful concepts and improves later recall}
\label{sec:recall}

SDA exceeds each model's own report in all 54 model--seed--budget
comparisons on the statedness-balanced pool.
At $k=2$, Qwen3-8B rises from 43.0\% to 53.7/54.8/52.6\%;
all six models improve in every seed (Table~\ref{tab:balanced}).
Across budgets, all three seeds reach exact McNemar $p<0.05$ in
17 of 18 model--budget cells. The 1--10 importance rating is the
strongest untrained admission policy on this pool for Qwen3-8B, and it
is evaluated for all six models at all three budgets. SDA exceeds it in
52 of 54
model--seed--budget comparisons; the two exceptions are Qwen3-14B at
three stored concepts, where two of three seeds reach 44.9\% against
the rating's 45.1\%.

On the statedness-balanced pool, Qwen3-8B SDA reaches 40.5/53.7/51.8\%
recall at $k=1,2,3$ (Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). The untrained
admission policies reach 35.8/48.5/50.7\% for the 1--10 rating,
31.4/44.6/47.7\% for the implied-content wording, 36.6/39.1/39.9\% for
the provenance-adjusted report, and 36.9/44.9/46.3\% for the combined
teacher used directly. Direct output likelihood reaches
34.7/41.9/41.6\%. The contributions of the teacher's two signals are
ablated in \S\ref{sec:ablations}.

\input{tables/tab_primary_policies}

At $k=2$, paired SDA gains are $+5.2$ points over the rating
($[+1.7,+8.8]$), $+9.1$ over implied-content wording ($[+5.7,+12.7]$),
$+14.6$ over the provenance-adjusted report ($[+9.5,+19.8]$), and
$+8.8$ over the direct combined teacher ($[+3.9,+13.9]$). These are
seed-averaged differences with 95\% intervals from 4,000 paired
episode-bootstrap draws; Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired} gives all budgets.

We implemented ConsistencyGate \citep{consistencygate2026}, A-MAC utility
\citep{amac2026} and Nemori's prediction-error criterion
\citep{nemori2026} from their papers, admitting top-$k$ candidates under
the same budget protocol (Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). On the primary,
statedness-balanced pool at two stored concepts, recall is 35.0\% for both
ConsistencyGate forms, 39.7\% for A-MAC utility and 25.9\% for prediction
error: all three methods fall below the model's own yes/no importance
report (43.0\%), while SDA reaches 53.7\%. Paired, seed-averaged SDA gains
are $+18.7$ points ($[+14.1,+23.6]$) over sampled ConsistencyGate, $+18.7$
($[+14.0,+23.5]$) over single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+14.1$
($[+9.3,+19.0]$) over A-MAC utility and $+27.8$ ($[+22.5,+33.2]$) over
prediction error, with 95\% bootstrap intervals over episodes from 4,000
draws. Every one of the 12 intervals for these methods in
Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired} excludes zero; the smallest gain is $+6.9$
($[+2.6,+11.1]$), over A-MAC utility at three stored concepts. ConsistencyGate and A-MAC utility track whether
the passage states a candidate: correlations on the statedness-balanced
pool are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for sampled
ConsistencyGate and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility. On the standard pool,
ConsistencyGate's own 0.7 threshold admits 26.7\% of useful candidates
against 70.8\% of all candidates; its within-episode useful-concept AUC is
0.133 for single-pass scoring and 0.190 for sampled scoring. These
independently proposed admission methods exhibit the same provenance
ordering identified in importance reports.


A fitted provenance offset reproduces the development pool's omission
regularity, while SDA improves admission across candidate pools.
Adding a single offset for stated candidates, fitted on development
episodes, raises the report's development AUC from 0.304 to 0.978 and
lowers its statedness-balanced AUC from 0.683 to 0.575. The fitted offset
orders every unstated candidate ahead of every stated candidate,
reproducing omission then report. Their recalls match at $k=2,3$;
at $k=1$, they are 36.6\% and 36.9\%. The offset learns which provenance
the benchmark rewards; SDA learns a passage-and-candidate admission judgment.

For Qwen3-8B at two stored concepts, retention separates stated and
inferred conditions: the yes/no report retains the useful concept in
46.4\% versus 33.0\% of episodes, the workspace readout in 92.8\% versus
35.7\%, and SDA in 76.8/81.8/72.4\% of stated episodes versus
64.8/64.8/64.3\% of inferred episodes across three seeds. SDA improves
inferred-concept retention over the workspace readout and retains stated
concepts. The 173 inferred-condition episodes contain no sentence naming
any candidate. On these episodes, SDA reaches 37.4/50.1/46.6\% recall at
$k=1,2,3$ against 24.9/40.5/37.0\% for the model's own yes/no report,
gains of $12.5$ ($[+6.7,+18.7]$), $9.6$ ($[+3.7,+15.6]$) and $9.6$
($[+4.4,+15.0]$) points. SDA exceeds the 1--10 rating by $5.6$ points at
two stored concepts ($[+0.8,+10.8]$) on these inferred-condition
episodes. Differences average three seeds with 95\% bootstrap intervals
over episodes from 4,000 draws.

Useful-concept retention carries the recall gain. On the standard
pool, Qwen3-8B within-episode AUC rises from 0.326 to 0.679--0.688
and retention from 29.8\% to 68.6--69.7\%
(Table~\ref{tab:episodemetrics}). Replaying SDA's two selections on
the 249 episodes where it retains the bridge, then replacing that
bridge with its next-ranked candidate, drops recall from 54.6\% to
37.8\%: a paired 16.9-point decrease, with 59 answers lost against
17 gained (exact McNemar $p=1.4\times10^{-6}$). The retained concept
supplies information the frozen reader uses.

Across six models on the naming-balanced pool, SDA exceeds the model's
own yes/no report in 17 of 18 model--budget cells, and the remaining cell
differs by 0.1 points (Table~\ref{tab:namingrecall}). SDA exceeds the
1--10 rating in all twelve model--budget cells at one and two stored
concepts; at three stored concepts, SDA and the rating are within 2.6
points in every model. Qwen3-8B within-episode AUC reaches 0.715--0.725
across seeds versus 0.566 for the report, and retention at $k=2$ reaches
53.2--54.0\% versus 24.0\% (Table~\ref{tab:namingbalanced}). The
admission gain persists when naming carries no utility information.

On the standard pool, all 18 runs exceed their own report, with a mean
9.6-point gain and exact McNemar $p\leq0.026$
(Tables~\ref{tab:sdabudget} and~\ref{tab:paired}). Both semantic and
string-match grading recover all 18 gains (App.~\ref{app:judge}).

\paragraph{Reader references and candidate proposal.}
Question-only and whole-passage readers reach 22.9\% and 63.9\% recall
on Qwen3-8B, bracketing SDA's 50.0\% on the standard pool.
With a context-only proposer, SDA reaches 35.5/33.3/33.1\% across
three seeds at $k=2$ (Table~\ref{tab:readerproposal}; proposal prompt
in App.~\ref{app:prompts}).

\subsection{Recall gains persist with unstated distractors}
\label{sec:budgets}

SDA improves recall when the useful inference competes with other
unstated concepts. All 54 model--seed--budget comparisons exceed their
model's report on the pool with unstated distractors
(Table~\ref{tab:stress}). On Qwen3-8B, direct output likelihood reaches
30.6/39.1/40.5\% recall at $k=1,2,3$, against SDA's
37.6/50.7/51.6\%; on the enlarged pool, the corresponding recalls
are 28.1/32.0/33.9\% versus 38.6/51.5/52.4\%
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:bigpool}). At $k=2$, SDA exceeds the
report by 9.6 points (paired 95\% CI $[5.5,13.8]$), the rating by
4.1 points ($[0.6,7.6]$), and omission then report by 5.8 points
($[0.8,10.8]$; Table~\ref{tab:paired}). With 12.41 candidates in the
enlarged pool, its corresponding gains are 11.0 points ($[6.7,15.2]$),
4.7 points ($[0.9,8.4]$), and 6.6 points ($[1.6,11.6]$).
At one stored concept, SDA trails omission then report by $8.4$ points
on the pool with unstated distractors ($[-12.7,-4.2]$) and by $5.5$
points on the enlarged pool ($[-9.6,-1.5]$) (Table~\ref{tab:paired}). At
two and three stored concepts, SDA leads every measured non-oracle
baseline on both pools (App.~\ref{app:poolresults}).

\subsection{Teacher composition and the admission objective}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:scale}

We ablate SDA's teacher by removing either the workspace readout or the
output likelihood and training the same adapter on the resulting labels,
holding episodes and step budget fixed. On the paired-passage battery
where only the passage identifies the useful concept, removing either
signal from SDA's teacher lowers answer recall.

Each candidate set appears twice, with two passages and their own
questions. The useful concept differs within each pair, so both episodes
include their partner's useful concept and only the passage identifies
which is useful. The 181 pairs are scored on Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B,
Qwen3-32B, Qwen2.5-7B and OLMo-2-7B, yielding 1,810 observations per
budget (Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}; per-model results:
Table~\ref{tab:crosspairmodels}). At one, two and three stored concepts,
the teacher without the workspace readout incurs recall costs of $+1.9$
($[+1.0,+2.8]$), $+0.6$ ($[-0.2,+1.4]$) and $+1.1$ ($[+0.4,+1.8]$)
points; retention costs are $+7.3$ ($[+6.1,+8.5]$), $+4.6$
($[+3.6,+5.7]$) and $+3.0$ ($[+2.0,+3.9]$) points. The teacher without the output
likelihood incurs recall costs of $+1.3$ ($[+0.3,+2.3]$), $+1.6$
($[+0.7,+2.5]$) and $+1.4$ ($[+0.5,+2.3]$) points; retention costs are
$-1.4$ ($[-2.9,+0.2]$), $+0.1$ ($[-1.3,+1.4]$) and $+1.7$
($[+0.6,+2.7]$) points. Costs are SDA minus the trained ablation,
averaged over three seeds and pooled over models. The 95\% intervals use
4,000 bootstrap draws resampling pairs and carrying each pair's rows
across all five models together.

\input{tables/tab_crosspair_pooled}

The replaced-passage test scores each episode with its own passage and
another episode's passage, keeping candidates, labels and question
fixed. Table~\ref{tab:replacedpassage} reports retention for Qwen3-8B on
363 standard-pool episodes with one stored concept. SDA's retention
drops from 48.3\% to 27.4\%, a decrease of $20.9$ points
($[+14.3,+27.4]$). For the student of the teacher without the workspace
readout, retention drops from 42.8\% to 39.9\%, or $2.9$ points
($[-3.5,+9.2]$). For the student of the teacher without the output
likelihood, it drops from 46.6\% to 20.1\%, or $26.5$ points
($[+20.5,+32.6]$). Without the workspace readout, the student's ordering
barely changes when the passage changes.

The primary pool combines five models and 1,815 observations per budget,
with each episode moving together across models in the bootstrap. The
teacher without the output likelihood incurs recall costs of $+1.6$
($[+0.7,+2.6]$) and $+1.7$ ($[+0.8,+2.7]$) points at one and two stored
concepts. The teacher without the workspace readout incurs costs of
$+0.7$ ($[-0.2,+1.6]$), $+0.6$ ($[-0.2,+1.4]$) and $+0.3$
($[-0.4,+1.1]$) points at one, two and three stored concepts.
Table~\ref{tab:teachermodels} gives per-model recall;
Table~\ref{tab:teachermodelpaired} gives paired intervals.

On the standard Fresh-2 pool at $k=2$, SDA reaches 50.0\% recall,
versus 45.7\% for the combined teacher used directly and 40.2--40.5\%
for the validation-selected log-odds objectives
(Tables~\ref{tab:sdaablation} and~\ref{tab:objective}).

Training the importance interface adds recall beyond changing its
wording. The \emph{implied-content wording} is the strongest measured
Qwen3-8B prompt baseline that permits inferences while leaving the
useful concept's provenance unspecified. It raises standard-pool report
AUC from 0.321 to 0.462 and recall at $k=1,2,3$ from
28.1/40.8/41.6\% to 29.2/41.3/44.9\%. Holding this wording fixed
for training and evaluation, SDA reaches 37.1/50.7/51.0\%, exceeding
the prompt baseline at every budget (exact McNemar
$p\leq6.9\times10^{-5}$; Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}).
Passage-only wording SDA reaches 37.6/50.0/52.2\%
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:paired}). The matched wording
comparison isolates the additional gain from learning the admission
judgment.

With teacher, episodes, adapter and step budget fixed, validation
within-episode admission AUC starts at 0.657 and reaches 0.824 at
the final step under SDA's vocabulary-normalized token objective.
Binary cross-entropy on the yes/no log-odds ends at
0.250, 0.333 and 0.301 across three seeds at learning rate
$10^{-5}$; validation selects the untrained checkpoint in every run.
At a step size five times smaller ($2\times10^{-6}$), selection
returns a trained checkpoint, whose standard-pool recall at $k=2$
is 40.2\%, against 50.0\% for SDA's token-level objective
(Table~\ref{tab:objective}).


\subsection{Standard serving preserves recall}
\label{sec:deployment}

Standard serving preserves SDA recall while scoring 242 candidates per
second on one A100-80GB (Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).
The served Qwen3-8B adapter achieves 50.1\% standard-pool recall, within
0.3 percentage points of the reference selections. Paired disagreements
are two versus three episodes (exact McNemar $p=1.0$); top-2 memories
agree on 91.7\% of episodes and admission AUC differs by at most 0.001.
Batched Qwen3-8B serving on the standard pool with prefix caching
on one A100-80GB reaches
242 candidates/s (4.1\,ms per candidate), against 290 candidates/s
(3.4\,ms) for the report, over 1,607 candidates after warm-up
(Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}). The adapter serves admission requests;
the frozen model answers from the admitted memory.
