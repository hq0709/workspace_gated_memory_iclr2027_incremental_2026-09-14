\section{The Admission Setting}
\label{sec:construction}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

An agent must select memories before the future question arrives.
Our setting makes a useful inference compete with concepts drawn from
a passage for a fixed number of memory slots. Each episode contains a
passage, an ordered candidate set, utility labels, and a future question
with its answer (Figure~\ref{fig:episode}). Only the passage and
candidates reach the admission policy.

\subsection{Episodes}

Each episode centers on a bridge concept: information that links the
passage to a future answer. \textbf{Decoupled} requires two inference
steps: recover the bridge from the passage, then combine it with
general knowledge to answer the future question. GPT-4.1 authors a
passage about a named person, together with the bridge, question, and
answer. The answer is excluded from the passage and candidate set.
The question connects to the passage through the person's name.

The author assigns the positive utility label to the intended bridge.
Vivid surface distractors and neutral passage concepts receive negative
utility labels. Mechanical filters check candidate strings, bridge and
answer exclusion, and question overlap. Deduplication removes repeated
concepts. A blind alias audit checks that recovering the bridge still
leaves an inference to the answer (App.~\ref{app:benchmarks}).

With two memory slots, the city in Figure~\ref{fig:episode} competes
with the elevator, gift shop, and scenery. \textbf{Explicit} states
the useful content. \textbf{Compositional} requires a multi-hop answer
and permits passage anchors in the question. \textbf{Fresh-1} and
\textbf{Fresh-2} repeat the Decoupled construction with new passages
and bridges absent from the training batteries. Each battery is frozen
before measurement.

The author uses the bridge and question to identify the intended
memory. The admission policy must predict its value before seeing
the question. Comparing admission policies changes the stored concepts
while keeping the future question and reader fixed.

\subsection{Candidate Pools and Controls}

The pools and controls separate the effects of passage mention,
candidate competition, context, authorship, and bridge category.

\paragraph{Mention and Position.}
The \textbf{stated-bridge control} appends \emph{The passage also mentions
bridge.} The \textbf{position control} places that sentence before
the passage. The \textbf{distractor-named control} substitutes a
distractor for the bridge in the appended sentence. Candidates, utility
labels, question, and answer stay fixed. These controls isolate the
named concept and sentence position.

\paragraph{Competition for Memory.}
Our primary \textbf{statedness-balanced pool} starts from the 363 held-out Fresh-2 episodes
in their frozen order. Counting from the first episode, odd positions
keep the passage unchanged, giving the inferred-concept condition.
Even positions append \emph{The passage also mentions bridge.},
with \emph{bridge} replaced by the useful concept. This gives the
stated-concept condition.

Every episode retains its original candidates, including the stated
distractors. Odd positions gain four unstated distractors; even
positions gain three. These are the first four or three additional
candidates, in order, from the pool with unstated distractors described
below. Both the naming intervention and the distractor count follow
position in the frozen order, with no sampling. The probe question
and answer, original utility labels, frozen reader, and blind judge
stay fixed. Added candidates receive negative utility labels.

The resulting 363 episodes average 7.93 candidates. Useful concepts
account for 13.3\% of stated candidates and 12.0\% of unstated
candidates. A statedness-only rule ranks near chance, with AUC
of 0.515 pooled and 0.516 within episodes.

The \textbf{naming-balanced pool} names four candidates in every
episode, using five sentence templates at varying positions.
Named candidates are useful 12.5\% of the time, against a 12.6\%
base rate.

These pools control different cues: statedness in the
statedness-balanced pool and intervention naming in the
naming-balanced pool. Their residual regularities point in opposite
directions. Intervention naming favors the useful concept in the
statedness-balanced pool. Overall statedness favors distractors in
the naming-balanced pool.

Two further pools increase competition for the same memory budget.
Both start from the standard Fresh-2 pool's 4.43 candidates per
episode. The \textbf{pool with unstated distractors} adds four
confusable concepts from the bridge's semantic category. An author
model proposes these concepts, which are filtered for absence from
the passage and original pool. The \textbf{enlarged pool} adds further
concepts from other episodes, reaching 12.41 candidates on average.
Both additions preserve the original passage, candidates, utility
labels, and future question while increasing the alternatives that
admission must rank.

\paragraph{Context, Authorship, and Bridge Category.}
These controls test whether admission depends on the paired passage,
the episode author, or the kind of useful concept.
\textbf{Shuffled context} replaces the passage with another episode's
passage while retaining its candidates and labels. The
\textbf{alternate-author benchmark} uses GPT-4o to write episodes with
an implied useful concept. The \textbf{entity-bridge benchmark} uses
GPT-5.6 to place a named-entity bridge among scene-object distractors
(App.~\ref{app:replications}).
