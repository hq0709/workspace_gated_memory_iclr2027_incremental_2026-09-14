\section{Set Construction and Leakage Audit}
\label{app:benchmarks}

\subsection{Passages, concepts and future questions}

The primary generator is GPT-4.1. Each episode contains a passage,
candidate concepts with gold utility labels, and a future question and
answer. Explicit places the useful concept in the passage.
\textbf{Implied-answer} training episodes cue an unstated useful concept
that supplies the answer, including episodes from the
alternate-generator set. Table~\ref{tab:appA1} reports this construction
as Implied-answer. Decoupled requires an unstated bridge to support a
further inference: the answer is absent from the passage and candidate
set, and the question shares no passage content word except a
protagonist's name. Compositional requires a multi-hop inference and
permits question anchors from the passage. Decoupled and Compositional
are development sets used for configuration selection and excluded from
gradient training.

Rule-based validation checks candidate strings, bridge absence, answer
exclusion and the Decoupled question's lexical overlap. Concept
deduplication removes repeated concepts across retained episodes, while
the leakage audit also checks full lexemes, inflected answers and
question anchors. A blind GPT-6-Astra alias audit receives the future
question, gold answer and bridge. It rejects identity, morphological or
derivational variants, synonyms, translations, abbreviations and
containment that trivially yields the answer. For example,
\emph{caterer/catering} is an alias, whereas \emph{Madrid/Spanish}
requires a further inference.

\subsection{Episode-generation prompt}
\label{app:authorprompt}
GPT-4.1 receives the system instruction, ``You are a careful dataset
designer for a cognitive-science experiment on LLM memory.'' The
following prose transcription preserves the generation constraints
(Prompt~\ref{prompt:author}); the output-format instruction requests one
structured record per episode. Generation uses temperature 1.0 and
batches of up to five episodes, after which rule-based filters and the
alias audit apply.

\subsection{Held-out and replication sets}
\label{app:fresh}

The interim held-out set and confirmation set use the Decoupled
construction prompt and rule-based filters, followed by deduplication
and disjointness filtering. A context match after lowercasing and
trimming against a prior set causes exclusion. A bridge that matches a
labeled useful concept of the training episodes also causes exclusion.
The confirmation set also excludes contexts appearing in the interim
held-out set. Shared answer categories, such as languages and
currencies, are permitted. The retained sets contain 316 and 363
episodes, respectively. The alternate-generator set repeats the
Implied-answer construction with GPT-4o, and the entity-bridge set comes
from GPT-5.6 (\S\ref{sec:construction}).

\subsection{Reconstructing the reported controls}
\label{app:pools}
\label{app:controls}

All pool interventions traverse the 363 confirmation-set episodes in
their fixed order and preserve the question, answer and original
utility labels. Added candidates receive negative utility labels.
Within an episode, original candidates precede additions, whose recorded
order is retained. The archived control file records the passages,
ordered additions, named candidates and passage permutation; replay uses
these recorded draws. The standard pool contains 1,607 candidates,
4.43 per episode. Statedness uses the case-insensitive stem match
specified in \S\ref{sec:setup}.

\paragraph{Stated-bridge, position and distractor-named controls.}
The exact sentence form is ``The passage also mentions \emph{concept}.''
The stated-bridge control appends this sentence with the useful concept,
whereas the position control prepends it. The distractor-named control
appends it with the first non-useful candidate in the original order.
Thus Yara's stated-bridge control adds ``The passage also mentions
insurance.'' after the passage, the position control puts that sentence
before ``Yara compared,'' and the distractor-named control instead
appends ``The passage also mentions insurer.''

\paragraph{Pool with unstated distractors.}
An LLM proposes four single-word concepts from the useful concept's
semantic category, asking for alternatives a careless reader might
confuse with it but that the passage does not imply. The instruction
requires lowercase words absent from the passage and a comma-separated
response. We retain the response order, discard a suggested concept that
matches an original candidate or occurs as a case-insensitive substring
of the passage, and append the accepted concepts. The pool contains
1,450 additions: four in 361 episodes and three in episodes 24 and 263
after filtering, giving 3,057 candidates in total (8.42 per episode).
For Yara, the ordered additions are \emph{warranty, mortgage, pension,
annuity}. The development confusable-distractor control applies the same
procedure to Decoupled, whereas the unrelated-distractor control takes
bridges from subsequent episodes, as described for the enlarged pool
below.

\paragraph{Statedness-balanced pool.}
Odd positions, counting from one, keep the original passage; even
positions append ``The passage also mentions \emph{concept}.'' with
the useful concept substituted. Every episode keeps its original
candidates and takes the first four accepted additions at odd positions
or the first three at even positions from the pool with unstated
distractors, preserving their order. This yields 2,877 candidates,
7.93 per episode. Yara occupies position one, so her passage is unchanged
and her pool is the five original candidates followed by \emph{warranty,
mortgage, pension, annuity}. Jibril occupies position two, so ``The
passage also mentions dubai.'' is appended to
Example~\ref{ex:jibril}'s passage and \emph{doha, riyadh, muscat} are
appended to its six candidates.

\paragraph{Naming-balanced pool.}
\label{app:namingpool}
This pool uses the same ordered candidate lists as the
statedness-balanced pool but starts from the original passages and
names four candidates per episode. At odd positions, the useful concept
is excluded and four other candidates are sampled uniformly without
replacement; at even positions, the useful concept is included and
three others are sampled. One Python standard pseudorandom generator
seeded with zero processes episodes in fixed order. The useful concept
is prepended to the sampled list when included, and each selected list
is shuffled before sentences are constructed. Sampling and shuffling
consume the same continuing random stream. The five sentence forms, in
rotation, are ``The passage also mentions \emph{concept}.'',
``\emph{concept} is relevant here.'', ``Note that \emph{concept} comes up
as well.'', ``There is also \emph{concept} to consider.'', and
``\emph{concept} features in this as well.''

Episodes and insertions are numbered from zero for the placement rule.
The first selected candidate uses the sentence form at the episode's
position in the five-form cycle; each subsequent candidate advances
one form. Trailing full stops are stripped from the original passage,
which is split at full-stop--space boundaries; each segment is trimmed
and one full stop restored to it. Before each insertion, the current
sentence list is counted, including earlier additions. With episode
index $e$, insertion index $j$ and current sentence count $n$, the
insertion boundary is $(e+3j)\bmod(n+1)$. Boundary zero precedes the
passage; the final boundary follows it.

For Yara, the shuffled selection is \emph{pension, annuity, insurer,
policy}. The additions are ``The passage also mentions pension.'',
``annuity is relevant here.'', ``Note that insurer comes up as well.'',
and ``There is also policy to consider.'' Their successive insertion
boundaries are zero, three, zero and two. The final passage begins with
the insurer, pension and policy sentences, then Yara's first two original
sentences, then the annuity sentence, then her final original sentence.
Across 2,877 candidates, there are 1,452 naming sentences, 181 of which
name the useful concept.

\paragraph{Enlarged pool.}
This pool combines the standard candidate list, the accepted same-topic
additions, and four bridges from other episodes. To choose the latter,
the procedure starts with the next episode in fixed order, advances
cyclically, and accepts a bridge only if it is absent from the passage,
absent from the original candidate list and distinct from earlier
accepted bridges. Selection stops after four accepted bridges. The three
lists are concatenated in this order, retaining the first occurrence of
each concept. The resulting 4,506 candidates average 12.41 per episode.
Yara's pool is \emph{insurer, deductible, history, policy, insurance,
warranty, mortgage, pension, annuity, dubai, church, kanji, doctor}.
The unrelated-distractor control uses just the standard candidates and
the four cross-episode additions; for Yara these are \emph{dubai,
church, kanji, doctor}.

\paragraph{Passage replacement and bridge replacement.}
Passage replacement uses the archived episode permutation, replacing
each passage while preserving its candidates, utility labels, question
and answer. The first episode receives the passage at position 105,
about Lars skating on slick tracks with thin-bladed boots; its candidates
remain Yara's five concepts and its answer remains \emph{agent}.
The bridge-replacement control acts on SDA's recorded top-two selections
only when they contain the useful concept: the other selection is
retained and the useful concept is replaced with the next-ranked
candidate, then the same frozen reader answers the same question again.
For a selection of \emph{dubai, elevator} with \emph{gift} next, the
replayed memory is \emph{gift, elevator}. The 249 measured replays apply
this rule to the recorded selections (\S\ref{app:episodemetrics}).

\paragraph{Candidate extraction and reader references.}
The context-only candidate extractor receives the original passage and
the instruction in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}; the future question and
bridge are hidden. Its parsed output replaces the supplied candidate
list in extraction order. For Yara the resulting candidates are
\emph{online, accident, hotline, premium, coverage, deductibles, payment,
policy}, so \emph{insurance} is unavailable to admission. At two stored
concepts, the same frozen reader answers from each policy's selected
pair. The closed-book reference gives that reader Yara's question with
no memory, whereas the full-context reference gives the same question
and her original three-sentence passage. Neither reference performs
admission.

\paragraph{Wording and replication controls.}
The wording controls change only the admission instruction, whose five
exact forms appear in Appendix~\ref{app:wording}. The alternate-generator
and entity-bridge sets repeat the episode construction with the
generators and concept categories of App.~\ref{app:replications}.

\paragraph{Naming-balanced ranking and retention.}

versus 24.0\% and 40.8\%, respectively.

\section{SDA Training and Scoring Details}
\label{app:training}

\subsection{Teacher construction and data split}

Within each episode, let $r_S(c)$ be a candidate's ascending mid-rank
under score $S$, with ties assigned the average of their occupied
ranks. For $m>1$ candidates, the percentile is
\begin{equation}
P_S(c;x)=\frac{r_S(c)-1}{m-1},
\qquad S\in\{W,\mathrm{LL}\}.
\label{eq:percentile}
\end{equation}
For a singleton pool the percentile is one half. Averaging the
percentiles yields the teacher in Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher}; descending
teacher score and then candidate order determine the top-2 labels.

A source group is one generator-and-construction group: Explicit
(88 episodes), implied-answer from the primary generator (75), or
implied-answer from the alternate generator (57). The split is
stratified by these groups and assigns whole episodes, including all
their candidates, to training or validation. Within each group,
episodes are sorted by a cryptographic hash of the fixed split seed
and the source-qualified episode identity; the lowest 20\%, rounded
up, enter validation. The split seed is zero and stays fixed across
optimization seeds. The three groups respectively contain 70 training
and 18 validation episodes, 60 training and 15 validation episodes,
and 45 training and 12 validation episodes, giving 175 training and
45 validation episodes in total. The selection protocol is specified
in \S\ref{sec:student}.

\subsection{Complete training configuration}
\label{app:configuration}

LoRA adapts the attention query, key, value and output projections and
the feed-forward gate, up and down projections. Base parameters remain
frozen. Only the target word contributes to the vocabulary-normalized
cross-entropy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}. Prompt tokens are masked. Qwen3
uses its chat template with thinking disabled.

\subsection{Measurement conventions}
\label{app:conventions}

The importance report in every main table is scored in log space over
the yes and no token sets. The wording comparison uses a common
scorer for all five wordings, so its rows are comparable under that
aggregation. Mistral-7B's passage-only wording gives AUC 0.411 in the
main tables and 0.440 in the wording comparison because the two
scorers aggregate differently. Scoring the same wording one prompt at
a time also gives 0.440, identical to the batched result.
On Qwen3-8B's standard pool, first-token workspace AUC is 0.543 at
the end of the passage. Appending the candidate and reading the state
immediately before its first token gives 0.530. The latter is the
matched baseline for full-candidate AUC 0.574.
The untrained report's answer accuracy is 40.8\% in the main tables
and 40.5\% in the objective ablation, which evaluates the untrained
checkpoint through the adapter-loading path instead of the frozen path.

\subsection{Admission baselines}
\label{app:baselines}

SDA is compared with the following baselines. The \textbf{importance
report} uses the untrained model's score $V$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}.
The \textbf{1--10 importance rating} is the expected rating under
normalized probabilities of the complete answer strings.
The \textbf{reversed importance report} admits the lowest-scoring
candidates. \textbf{Unstated-first} prioritizes candidates absent from
the passage under the statedness test and breaks ties at random.
\textbf{Unstated-first with report ordering} prioritizes unstated
candidates and uses the importance report to order candidates within
each statedness class. The \textbf{statedness-adjusted report} adds a
single coefficient times the candidate's statedness indicator to the
untrained report, with the coefficient fitted on development episodes
and held fixed for evaluation. The \textbf{combined teacher used
directly} ranks by Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher} without training an adapter.
The \textbf{implied-content wording} permits implied content; the
\textbf{statedness-cued wording} explicitly says the passage may never
mention the useful concept (App.~\ref{app:wording}).
\textbf{Random selection} samples candidates uniformly; \textbf{output
likelihood} and the \textbf{workspace readout} rank candidates directly
by their respective scores. \textbf{Gold-concept selection} ranks the
labeled useful concept first; the closed-book and full-context readers
provide accuracy references.

\subsection{Published admission criteria as implemented}
\label{app:publishedbaselines}

\paragraph{ConsistencyGate.}
ConsistencyGate \citep{consistencygate2026} is a write-time gate that
scores how strongly the source context supports a candidate fact. We
measure two forms. The sampled form follows their Equations~2 and~3,
using their Appendix~C prompt verbatim with five samples at temperature
one and at most eight new tokens per sample. Its score is the mean of
the parsed decimals. The single-pass form follows their Equation~4
variant: the same question is posed as a yes/no question, and the score
is the probability of yes from a softmax over the yes and no token
log-probabilities. Ranking uses the score itself under the shared
top-$k$ budget, and their 0.7 threshold enters only the gate-rate
diagnostic.

\paragraph{A-MAC.}
A-MAC \citep{amac2026} is a multi-factor value model. Utility is obtained
from one temperature-zero call asking whether the candidate is
actionable, supports likely follow-up questions, or captures a
persistent constraint or preference. Confidence is the maximum ROUGE-L
between the candidate and a supporting passage sentence. The type prior
is their rule-based entity cue. Their novelty term compares against
memory already held, and their recency term decays with elapsed time.
Within an episode, memory is empty at the first admission and all
candidates share one timestamp, so neither term orders candidates.
These two terms are therefore omitted from the ranking score. The
three remaining factors are combined as a weighted sum, with the
utility weight fixed at one. Confidence and type-prior weights are
searched on a grid from 0.0 to 2.0 in steps of 0.1, selected by
within-episode AUC inside five-fold cross-validation over the 363
episodes, and evaluated out of fold. Every fold selects zero weight
for both confidence and type prior, so the fitted combination coincides
with utility alone. Out-of-fold within-episode AUC is 0.6795 for both.

\paragraph{Nemori.}
Nemori's prediction-error criterion \citep{nemori2026} keeps what the
model fails to predict. Applied to a candidate concept and its
passage, the score is the candidate's surprisal under the passage, the
negative of the output likelihood. The criterion ranks candidates for
admission, the part of Nemori that applies to this setting; memories
already held play no role.

All three score the same unit as every other policy, a candidate concept
and its episode passage with the future question hidden, and admit the
top-$k$ under the same budget protocol. Prompts~\ref{prompt:cgsample}
to~\ref{prompt:amac} give the three scoring instructions.

\subsection{Importance prompt, reader, rating and extraction scoring}
\label{app:prompts}

Appendix~\ref{app:promptlist} lists the prompts. Each is a user message
rendered with the model's chat template and assistant generation prefix;
Qwen3 thinking is disabled. SDA and the importance report share the
importance prompt (Prompt~\ref{prompt:admission}).

The affirmative token set contains the distinct first tokens of
\emph{yes} in lowercase, initial-capital and uppercase forms, with
and without a leading space. The negative set is constructed analogously
from \emph{no}. Equation~\eqref{eq:score} gives the common report
score; SDA applies the same scoring rule with its adapter enabled.

The reader (Prompt~\ref{prompt:reader}) uses the frozen model with its
admission adapter disabled.
The closed-book reference asks ``Answer this question concisely.''
followed by the question and answer prefix. The full-context reference
supplies the passage, then ``Answer concisely.'' and the same question
and answer prefix. Greedy decoding is used throughout.

Let $a_r$ be the complete answer string for rating $r$, and let $u_R(c,x)$
be the rating prompt (Prompt~\ref{prompt:rating}). Each answer receives
its full sequence probability, multiplying the conditional probabilities
of its tokens. The score is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
R(c;x)&=\sum_{r=1}^{10}r\,\pi_r(c;x),\\
\pi_r(c;x)&=\frac{p_{\mathcal M}(a_r\mid u_R(c,x))}
{\sum_{j=1}^{10}p_{\mathcal M}(a_j\mid u_R(c,x))}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:rating}
\end{equation}
A rating represented by multiple tokens is scored as a complete answer.

The context-only candidate extractor (Prompt~\ref{prompt:proposal}) uses
Qwen3-8B with greedy decoding. Distinct parsed concepts form the
candidate pool. The useful concept, future question and answer are
withheld from extraction and carried forward for evaluation.
Useful-concept coverage accepts case differences and a trailing plural
ending.

\subsection{Ablation protocol}
\label{app:ablationprotocol}

The teacher and optimization ablations in \S\ref{sec:ablations} use the
same training episodes, adapter shape and matched step budget. The
accuracy-reward condition samples admitted sets from the admission
scores and asks the frozen reader to answer from the selected memory.
Answer correctness supplies the reward. Within each rollout group,
reward differences are normalized into advantages; updates use a
clipped group-relative policy objective and a penalty for departure
from the reference policy \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}. The future
question enters the reward computation. The refinement condition
applies these updates after SDA training.
\section{Held-Out Set Locking}
\label{app:freeze}

The interim held-out set and the confirmation set were locked on
15 September 2026. Each set's episode count and cryptographic digest
were recorded before evaluated models read it: 316 episodes for the
interim held-out set and 363 for the confirmation set. The interim
held-out set supplied the initial held-out evaluation, while the

\section{Answer Grading and Serving Protocol}
\label{app:repro}

\subsection{Blind semantic judge}
\label{app:judge}

The judge, GPT-6-Astra, receives the question, gold answer and reader
response with no admission-policy identity. Prompt~\ref{prompt:grading}
quotes the substantive grading instruction verbatim; its output-format
instruction requests a label for every supplied answer.
Only correct responses contribute to answer accuracy; hedged and
incorrect responses count as errors. Judgments are reused for identical
question, gold answer and response triples. Each answer is also graded
by string matching after normalization. Agreement is 85.1\% across
13,068 answers; every training run exceeds its own report under both
graders.

\subsection{Amortized scoring time and throughput}
\label{app:timing}

The timing evaluation loads Qwen3-8B on one A100-80GB and constructs an
importance prompt for each of the 1,607 standard-pool candidates. vLLM
enables prefix caching and LoRA serving, warms the engine on an initial
subset, and times batched generation over the full candidate list.
Each prompt generates one token greedily; SDA attaches the adapter to
the admission request, while the importance report uses the same engine
without it. Model loading is outside the timed region. Milliseconds per
candidate are wall time divided by candidate count; throughput is
candidate count divided by wall time. Reference selections are the
primary SDA seed's selections in the main evaluation; both served and
reference selections are passed to the same frozen reader and semantic
judge. The serving engine recovers yes/no masses from returned token
log probabilities.

\subsection{Capability with the adapter enabled on every token}
\label{app:capability}

Enabling the adapter on every token changes the four measured capability
scores by at most 0.3 percentage points under matched prompts.
Base and adapter scores are
71.6\% and 71.5\% on MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}, 80.4\% and
80.7\% on ARC-Easy, 56.2\% and 56.4\% on ARC-Challenge

\subsection{Pooled AUC and comparison-pair composition}
\label{app:composition}

On the confirmation set, the useful concept is stated in 5\% of
episodes and negative candidates in 99\%; in 95\% of episodes, the
useful concept is the sole unstated candidate. Adding unstated
distractors leaves every original Qwen3-8B report score unchanged
while adding 1,450 negative candidates to the original 1,244 and
retaining the same 363 positives. Report AUC against the added
negatives is 0.889, compared with 0.322 against the original negatives.
The pooled AUC is therefore the weighted average
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{expanded}}
&=\frac{1244}{2694}\,\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{original}}\\
&\quad+\frac{1450}{2694}\,\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{added}}\\
&=0.627.
\end{split}
\label{eq:pooldecomposition}
\end{equation}
The added comparisons, which account for 53.8\% of all
positive--negative pairs, raise AUC without changing the ordering
of the original candidates. At a two-concept budget, the report
continues to prioritize passage content: answer accuracy is 41.0\%,
below SDA's accuracy (App.~\ref{app:poolresults};
Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).

\subsection{Importance wordings}
\label{app:wording}

Five matched wordings keep the episode, candidates and gold utility
labels fixed. They ask about passage-only importance, remove that
restriction, allow implied content (\emph{implied-content wording}),
state that the passage may omit the useful concept
(\emph{statedness-cued wording}), or specify a two-concept budget.
The measurements cover four models on the standard pool and the
pool with unstated distractors (Table~\ref{tab:wording}).
On Qwen3-32B, the statedness-cued wording raises standard-pool AUC
from 0.398 to 0.876; the corresponding Qwen3-8B values are 0.321 and
0.537. On Mistral-7B, the implied-content wording gives
0.518, compared with 0.440 for passage-only importance. The direction
and magnitude of the wording effect vary across models.

The statedness-cued wording's ranking changes when the useful concept
is stated (\S\ref{sec:gap}). Its standard-pool answer accuracy on
Qwen3-8B is 32.2\%, 43.0\% and 46.0\% at $k=1,2,3$
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

The implied-content wording permits inferences without specifying

44.9\% at $k=1,2,3$. All SDA seeds exceed the wording alone at every

For wording $j$ with prompt $u_j(c,x)$, the scorer is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
s_j(c;x)={}&\log\!\sum_{t\in Y_{\mathrm{word}}}p_{\mathcal M}(t\mid u_j(c,x))\\
&-\log\!\sum_{t\in N_{\mathrm{word}}}p_{\mathcal M}(t\mid u_j(c,x)).
\end{split}
\label{eq:wordingscore}
\end{equation}

prompts. The importance report in the main tables instead uses the

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{app:objective}

The objective baseline trains the admission score directly by applying
binary cross-entropy to the yes/no log-odds in Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}.
Teacher labels, training episodes, adapter shape and the 414-step
budget are fixed. Within-episode AUC on the validation split starts at
0.657 for the untrained model and reaches 0.824 at the final step under
SDA's vocabulary-normalized token objective.
At learning rate $10^{-5}$, the log-odds objective ends at
0.250, 0.333 and 0.301 across the three seeds. Validation selects the
untrained checkpoint in all three runs (Table~\ref{tab:objective}).

At a step size five times smaller ($2\times10^{-6}$), validation
selects a trained checkpoint after 100 steps, with within-episode AUC
0.690 and standard-pool answer accuracy of 40.2\% at $k=2$, against
50.0\% for SDA's vocabulary-normalized token prediction.
At $5\times10^{-6}$, selection returns the untrained checkpoint.
Each smaller-step-size run uses one seed. SDA's token objective yields
9.8 points higher answer accuracy than the trained log-odds checkpoint
and 9.6 points higher than the untrained checkpoint selected at the
other learning rates.
