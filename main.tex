\title{Remember What You Thought, Not What You Said:\\
Self-Distilled Memory Admission for LLM Agents}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LLM agents must choose memories before future questions arrive, although
useful concepts are often unstated inferences. Importance judgments favor
candidates the passage states: the importance report distinguishes
useful concepts among unstated candidates alone but falls below chance
on the standard pool (the generator's original candidate lists), where
useful concepts are usually unstated. The model's workspace readout
(a candidate's peak reciprocal rank under the logit lens across layers)
ranks useful concepts above chance on this pool. Self-Distilled Admission
(SDA) labels the top two candidates by the workspace readout and output
likelihood and, through the model's importance prompt, trains a LoRA
adapter that is used only for admission requests. Gradient updates use
only labels the frozen model generates, with no future question or reward.
Across six models, SDA improves answer accuracy over each model's own
report in 54 of 54 model--seed--budget comparisons on the primary
statedness-balanced pool.
Qwen3-8B gains 10.7 points at two stored concepts; published admission
criteria (ConsistencyGate, A-MAC, Nemori) score below the untrained report.
Removing the workspace readout from SDA's teacher lowers retention of
unstated useful concepts in all five ablated models.
An off-the-shelf serving engine runs the adapter at 242 candidates per
second. The model internally encodes concepts worth storing that its
importance report does not reflect; SDA trains the model's answer to its
importance prompt to admit those concepts.
\end{abstract}
