\title{Remember What You Thought, Not What You Said:\\
Self-Distilled Memory Admission for LLM Agents}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An agent must choose what to store before a future question arrives,
and discarding a useful inference can cost it the answer.
We find that the ordering of an importance report, a model's yes/no
judgment of what to remember, follows its candidate pool's provenance
structure: across six models, useful-concept AUC is 0.322--0.427 on the
standard pool, where the useful concept is usually unstated, and
0.658--0.736 when the useful concept is equally likely to be stated or
unstated.
Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) ranks candidates with a frozen model's
workspace readout and output likelihood, labels the top two, and trains
one adapter on those labels through the model's own importance prompt.
On the primary statedness-balanced pool, SDA improves Qwen3-8B's answer
recall by 10.7 percentage points over its own report and 5.2 points over
the 1--10 importance rating at two stored concepts, and exceeds each
model's own report in 54 of 54 model--seed--budget comparisons.
All three seeds reach exact McNemar $p<0.05$ in 17 of 18 model--budget cells.
The served Qwen3-8B adapter achieves 50.1\% recall on the standard
pool at 242 candidates/s.
These results make admission a trainable component of agent memory,
using a model's own computation to preserve useful inferences for
future questions.
\end{abstract}
